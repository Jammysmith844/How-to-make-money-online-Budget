How the calculation works

This calculator turns a rate and a schedule into a realistic picture of what a side hustle pays. It applies four simple steps:

  1. Gross income — rate × hours (or rate per project × projects per month, or fixed monthly income).
  2. Subtract expenses — tools, materials, fuel, subscriptions, and any other recurring cost.
  3. Subtract estimated tax — a flat percentage you choose, applied to the profit.
  4. Divide by actual hours worked — including prep and admin — to get your real effective hourly rate.

The effective hourly number is often the most useful output. A gig that pays $30/hour but takes 2 hours of prep for every 1 hour billed is really paying $10/hour.

Worked examples

Freelance writing

$40/hour, 8 hours per week, $25/month in software, 15% tax. Gross yearly is about $16,640. After expenses and tax, net is roughly $13,960 — about $13.42/hour after everything.

Weekend driving / delivery

$18/hour, 12 hours per week, $120/month in fuel and maintenance, 10% tax. Gross yearly is about $11,232. Net after costs lands near $8,570. The effective hourly rate drops once fuel is accounted for.

Small Etsy shop

Fixed $600/month revenue, $180/month in materials and fees, 20% tax. Net is around $4,032/year. If you spend 15 hours per week on it, that works out to about $5.17/hour — a useful reality check.

Good to know before you commit

  • Count every hour. Prep, admin, invoicing, and travel are real work. If you only count billed hours, your effective rate will look better than it is.
  • Track expenses from day one. A separate bank account or a simple spreadsheet makes tax time far easier.
  • Tax rates vary widely. Side income is often taxed at your marginal rate, which can be higher than you expect. Ask an accountant if you're unsure.
  • Fixed monthly income isn't always passive. If it requires ongoing work, divide by the hours it takes to keep it running.
  • Scale matters. Doubling hours doesn't always double net income if expenses scale with volume.

Frequently asked questions

What tax rate should I use?

Use your marginal income tax rate plus any self-employment or social contributions that apply in your country. If you don't know it, start with 15% as a conservative placeholder and adjust once you've spoken to an accountant.

Should I include travel time?

Yes, if the travel is required for the work. A delivery job that pays for 8 hours but takes 10 door-to-door is really paying you for 10 hours of your day. Including it gives a truer effective hourly rate.

Why is my effective hourly rate so much lower than my headline rate?

Because the headline rate usually only counts billed time. Once you subtract unbilled prep, admin, expenses, and tax, the real figure drops. This is normal and is the main reason people misjudge side-hustle income.

Does this calculator handle multiple income streams?

Not directly. Run it once per stream to get a per-stream picture, then add the net monthly figures together for a combined number. Keeping streams separate also makes it easier to see which ones are actually worth your time.

Does the calculator store my numbers?

No. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, and closing the tab wipes the inputs.

Limitations

  • The tax field is a simple flat percentage. It does not model brackets, deductions, credits, or country-specific rules.
  • Currency selection changes the symbol only. No exchange rates are applied.
  • Expenses are treated as a flat monthly figure — they don't scale with hours.
  • The calculator does not produce tax documents or reports. For filing, use dedicated accounting software or speak to an accountant.
